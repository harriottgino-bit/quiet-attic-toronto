Scratching in the attic at night: is it a squirrel or a raccoon?

It’s 2 a.m. and something is moving above your bedroom. Here’s how to tell what it is — and what to do before morning.

You don’t need to see the animal to narrow it down. The time you hear it, and the kind of sound it makes, tells you most of what you need to know.

If it’s light, quick scratching and scampering — usually early morning or during the day — it’s most likely squirrels

Squirrels are daytime animals. You’ll hear them at dawn, and again before they settle for the night. The sound is fast and light: little feet running, scratching in short bursts, sometimes a rolling sound like a small ball. They often get in through a roof vent, a gap in the soffit, or where the roof meets the wall.

If it’s heavy thumping, dragging or rolling — usually after dark — it’s most likely raccoons

Raccoons are night animals, and they’re much bigger. The sound is heavier: a loud thump, something being dragged, a slow rolling noise across the ceiling. If it sounds like someone is walking around up there, that’s a raccoon, not a squirrel.

Try the 20-second sound check on our home page

Stand under the noisiest spot, listen for 20 seconds, and note three things: what time it is, whether the sound is light and quick or heavy and slow, and whether it stops and starts or keeps going. That tells us — and the person who comes out — almost everything.

Tonight, before anyone comes out

Do

  • Note the time and the kind of sound, and check outside in daylight for a hole near the roofline. Take a photo if you can do it safely from the ground.
  • Keep the attic hatch closed and keep pets away from it.

Please don’t

  • Go up into the attic at night, and don’t try to trap anything yourself. A cornered animal bites, and in Ontario a trapped wild animal has to be released close by — within 1 km, and within 24 hours — so trapping it yourself doesn’t solve the problem anyway.
  • Block the hole yet. If babies are inside, sealing the entry traps them there.

Questions people ask

How can I tell if it’s a squirrel or a raccoon?

Listen for when you hear it and how heavy it sounds. Light, quick scratching and scampering in the early morning or during the day points to squirrels. Heavy thumping, dragging or rolling after dark points to raccoons.

Should I block the hole tonight?

No. If babies are inside, sealing the entry traps them there. Note the time and the kind of sound, and check outside in daylight for a hole near the roofline.

Should I go into the attic or try to trap it myself?

No. Don’t go up into the attic at night, and don’t try to trap anything yourself. A cornered animal bites, and in Ontario a trapped wild animal has to be released close by — within 1 km, and within 24 hours.
