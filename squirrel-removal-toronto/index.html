Squirrels in the attic

the 6 a.m. alarm clock you never set

It starts as a few scratches. Then it's running. Then one morning you realize you can follow something's entire commute across your ceiling. Squirrels are daytime animals, so if your attic comes alive at sunrise and settles at dusk, this is almost certainly your guest — and the good news is they're one of the most straightforward removals there is, done kindly.

How you'll know it's squirrels

You don't need to climb up and confirm it — these signs, noticed from your bed and your driveway, are plenty.

  • Scratching, scampering, or a rolling sound (that's a nut being moved) — mostly early morning and late afternoon
  • Quiet all night, every night: squirrels sleep when you do
  • A chewed roof vent, soffit, or fascia, or a small hole right at the roof edge
  • The same squirrel running the same roofline, or dropping onto the roof from the same tree branch
  • Leaves and shredded insulation gathered into a nest, if anyone's peeked into the attic
  • Chewed wiring or wood up there — squirrels chew constantly, and this is the sign that makes it urgent

Getting them out, kindly

No traps, no poison, no drama. Four steps, and the squirrels do most of the work themselves.

1

A proper look, first

The pro inspects the attic and roofline to confirm it's squirrels, find every way in — there's usually more than one — and check for pups. Baby squirrels can't leave on their own, and finding them now changes the plan for the better.

2

The one-way door

A small door goes over the main entry: out is easy, back in is impossible. The squirrels leave to feed, as they do every day — and that evening, home is closed. Every other entry gets sealed with chew-proof screening at the same visit.

3

If there are babies

Pups are placed safely outside, right by the entry, in a warm box. Their mother finds them — she is listening for them — and carries them one by one to a backup nest, usually within hours. Nobody is separated for good.

4

Sealed, and quiet again

Once the attic is confirmed clear, the door comes off and the entry is sealed for good. If nesting shredded your insulation, cleanup or replacement can be quoted then — only if it's actually needed.

Squirrel questions, answered straight

Why not just trap and release them?

Because Ontario law says a captured animal must be released within 1 km of capture, within 24 hours — and a squirrel released a few streets away treats it as a short commute. It's home by dinner. The one-way door plus sealed entries is what actually ends it.

It's spring and I think there are babies. What happens?

That's the most common situation of all, and the process is built for it: mum leaves through the one-way door, the pups wait safely in a reunion box by the entry, and she relocates them herself. The final seal-up simply waits until the attic is confirmed empty. Mention babies in the form — there's a field for exactly that.

Will they just chew a brand-new hole?

They'll try the old spots first — which is why every entry, not just the main one, gets sealed in chew-proof material. Trimming branches that bridge to your roof removes their favourite highway, and your pro will point out which ones matter.

How fast do I need to act?

This week rather than this month. Squirrels' teeth never stop growing, so neither does the chewing — and wiring damage is a genuine fire risk. If you're hearing activity daily, pick “hearing activity daily” in the form and mean it.

Scratching in the attic? Start here

If you're hearing light scratching, scurrying or rolling sounds in the morning or early evening, and it's coming from above your ceiling, squirrels are the usual suspect. They get in through roof vents, soffits and small gaps along the roofline, and they love a warm, dry attic — especially in spring, when there may be babies up there too.

Tonight, don't seal the hole while they're still inside, and don't try to trap one yourself. A sealed-in squirrel will chew a new way out, and a trapped one has to be released close by under Ontario's rules. The kind way out is a one-way door: they can leave, they can't get back in, and any babies are reunited with their mother outside.

When you call, it helps to say when you hear it, where in the house it sounds loudest, and whether you've seen anything near the roof. That's what shapes the visit — and the price is confirmed after inspection, never guessed over the phone.
