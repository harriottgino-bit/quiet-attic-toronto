Skunks under the deck

you smelled this guest before you saw it

Skunks are the ground-floor tenants. They don't climb into attics — they dig a quiet, dark den under a deck, porch, shed, or set of concrete steps, come out at night to hunt grubs in your lawn, and would honestly rather be left alone. The smell is the headline, but a spray is a skunk's last resort, not its hobby. Handled calmly — family kept together, den screened so it stays empty — this is routine, humane work, and nobody has to get sprayed doing it. Least of all your dog.

How you'll know it's a skunk

With skunks, your nose usually knows first. These signs, noticed around the yard and the deck, are plenty — no need to go peering under anything.

  • A strong, musky skunk smell hanging around the deck, porch, or yard — strongest at night and early morning, even when nothing has actually sprayed
  • A dug opening under a deck, porch, shed, or concrete steps, with loose soil fanned out beside it
  • Small cone-shaped holes dug in the lawn overnight — a skunk hunting grubs and insects just under the surface
  • A slow, waddling black-and-white shape crossing the yard at dusk, unbothered and in no hurry at all
  • Your dog suddenly obsessed with one gap in the deck or fence line, especially after dark
  • In late spring and early summer: a line of small kits trailing their mother across the yard at dusk

Getting them out, kindly

Same humane idea as the attic animals, adapted to a ground den: the skunks leave on their own at night, the family stays together, and the den is screened so the next skunk doesn't move into the vacancy.

1

A careful look, first

The pro confirms it's a skunk den, finds every opening in and out, and checks for kits. Skunk kits are born in late spring and can't follow their mother out for their first weeks — finding them now decides the timing, so nobody gets sealed in and nobody gets separated.

2

Every opening but one, closed

All the den's entrances are screened shut except the main one. That way there's exactly one way out — through the door in the next step — and no side door back in.

3

The one-way door on the den

A one-way door goes over the main opening: out is easy, back in is impossible. The skunk heads out at dusk to feed, as it does every night, and comes home to a closed door. If there are kits old enough to follow, they trail out behind her; if they're still too young, the door simply waits until they are — the family leaves together, or the job waits.

4

Screened against digging

Once the den is confirmed empty, the door comes off and the real fix goes in: heavy-gauge screening trenched down and out around the deck, porch, or shed — an underground barrier a skunk can't dig past. An open, unscreened den is a vacancy sign to the next skunk; screening is what makes this a one-time fix. Securing bins and pet food, and dealing with a grub-heavy lawn, removes the other invitations.

Until the visit: don't get sprayed

A skunk sprays when it's cornered or rushed — almost every spray story involves a dog charging in. A calm yard is a smell-free yard.

Do this

  • Give it a wide berth. Skunks are slow, short-sighted, and genuinely not looking for trouble. If you see one, just back away and let it waddle off.
  • Check the yard before letting the dog out at night. Light on, quick look, then out. Keep dogs leashed after dark while a den is active.
  • Watch for the warning signs. Foot-stamping, hissing, and a raised tail mean "back off" — a skunk would rather warn you than spray.
  • Take away the buffet. Secure green bins, bring pet food in, and clear fallen fruit — an easy food source is half the reason a yard gets chosen.

Please don't

  • Don't corner, chase, or try to shoo it off. That's exactly what turns a calm skunk into a spray — usually right where you live.
  • Don't block the den opening yourself. You could trap kits — or a very annoyed adult — under your own deck, and the smell problem gets far worse.
  • Don't set your own trap. A trapped skunk is a stressed skunk, Ontario law requires release within 1 km within 24 hours anyway, and an unscreened den just gets a new tenant.
  • Don't use mothballs, ammonia rags, or poison. They don't evict skunks, they're a hazard to pets and kids, and they make the den area smell worse, not better.

Skunk questions, answered straight

Will it spray my dog or my kids?

Almost certainly not, if nobody rushes it. Spraying is a last resort — a skunk only carries so much, and it would rather stamp its feet, hiss, and waddle away. Nearly every spray incident is a dog charging straight in. Keep dogs leashed or checked at night while the den is active, and the risk stays very low.

It's spring and I think there are kits under there. What happens?

That's the most common skunk situation of all, and the timing is built around it. If the kits are still too young to follow their mother out, the one-way door waits until they can — then the whole family leaves together at dusk. Nothing is sealed while anything is still inside. Mention kits in the form — there's a field for exactly that.

Something already got sprayed. What do I do about the smell?

Air the area out first — most outdoor spray smell fades over days to a couple of weeks. For a sprayed dog, use a pet-safe de-skunking wash (tomato juice mostly just masks it). If the spray happened right at the den under your deck, odour cleanup under the structure can be quoted after inspection, along with the removal.

Why not just trap it and let it go somewhere else?

Because Ontario law says a captured animal must be released within 1 km of capture, within 24 hours — close enough that it can simply come back. And even if it didn't, the open den under your deck would be taken by the next skunk within the season. The one-way door plus digging-proof screening is what actually ends it.

Smelling skunk under the deck? Start here

If there's a strong skunk smell near your deck, porch or shed — especially in the evening — and you can see a dug-out hollow or a small hole under the structure, a skunk has probably moved in underneath. They're nocturnal, they dig, and they love the shelter of a low deck.

Tonight, keep pets inside after dark, don't block the hole, and don't try to flush one out with a hose or a light. A scared skunk sprays, and a blocked-in skunk sprays too. The kind way out is a one-way door under the deck, so it can leave on its own and can't get back in — then the gap is sealed.

When you call, it helps to say where you smell it, when it's strongest, and whether you've seen the skunk or its digging. That's what shapes the visit — and the price is confirmed after inspection, never guessed over the phone.
